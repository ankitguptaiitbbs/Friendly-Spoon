# 🥙 Souvlaki vs. 🍢 Kalamaki

*A Game of Parity, Order, and Inevitable Fate*

---

> Some games are won by skill.
> Some by luck.
> And some are decided **before the first move is made**.

This is one of those games.

---

## 🎲 The Arena

We begin with a **sequence of integers** — a row of skewers laid out on the grill.
Two players stand over it:

* 🥙 **Souvlaki** — plays on **odd rounds**
* 🍢 **Kalamaki** — plays on **even rounds**

There will be exactly **n − 1 rounds**.

On round `i`, the active player may:

* ⏭️ *Do nothing* and move on, or
* 🔁 *Swap* `a[i]` with `a[i+1]`

At the end of all rounds, destiny is checked:

* ✅ If the array is **non‑decreasing**, Souvlaki wins
* ❌ Otherwise, Kalamaki wins

But here’s the twist 👀

> **Before the game even starts**, Souvlaki may reorder the array however he likes.

The real question becomes:

> Can Souvlaki arrange the board so that **no matter how Kalamaki plays**, victory is guaranteed?

---

## 🧠 The Insight (The Game Behind the Game)

This isn’t about clever swaps mid‑game.

This is about **control**.

Rounds alternate.
Power alternates.
And crucially…

> **Some positions are forever out of reach.**

Once the initial order is set:

* On **odd indices** → Souvlaki can interfere
* On **even indices** → Kalamaki gets the final say

So if *any disorder* exists at a position Kalamaki controls…

🧨 Souvlaki can never fix it.

---

## 🔍 The Winning Condition

Souvlaki’s only safe move is **prevention**.

He sorts the array first — perfectly, calmly, deterministically.

Then he inspects:

> Every **even‑controlled boundary** must already be stable.

In concrete terms:

* After sorting,
* For every index `i` where `i` is **odd (0‑based)**:

  * `a[i]` must be **equal** to `a[i+1]`

Why?

Because Kalamaki controls that boundary.
If two different numbers sit there… 🍢 wins.

---

## 🧪 The Ritual (Translated into Code)

```cpp
sort(nums.begin(), nums.end());

for(int i = 0; i < n-1; i++){
    if(i % 2 && nums[i] != nums[i+1]){
        cout << "NO";
        return;
    }
}
cout << "YES";
```

No simulation.
No backtracking.
No minimax.

Just **parity** and **inevitability**.

---

## ⚖️ The Final Verdict

| Situation                               | Outcome              |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| All Kalamaki-controlled pairs are equal | 🥙 **Souvlaki wins** |
| Any such pair differs                   | 🍢 **Kalamaki wins** |

The game is solved **before it begins**.
